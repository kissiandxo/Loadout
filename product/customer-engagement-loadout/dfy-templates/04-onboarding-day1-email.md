# DFY Template #4 — Onboarding Day-1 Email

**Use when:** A new customer just signed up / made their first purchase. This is the highest-leverage single email in the entire customer lifecycle — most onboarding success or failure is determined here.

**Channel:** Email
**Length:** Short (under 100 words for SaaS, under 150 for e-com)
**Calibrated for:** ONE clear action that proves value within 5 minutes of opening

---

## Variant A — SaaS / subscription new signup

**Subject:** Your first 5 minutes with [Product]

**Body:**

> Hi [Name],
>
> Glad you're in.
>
> Most teams get value out of [Product] the moment they [do the specific aha-action — e.g., create their first project, save their first item, add their first teammate].
>
> Want to do that now? One click: [link directly to the aha-action with their account in context]
>
> Should take about 60 seconds. I'll send the next thing tomorrow — for today, just the one action.
>
> — [Founder name], [Title]

---

**Why this works:**
- Lead with the value frame ("most teams get value when…")
- ONE action, not five
- Direct link with account in context (no "log in first then navigate to…")
- Time estimate ("60 seconds") removes friction
- "I'll send the next thing tomorrow" — signals there's a sequence, but doesn't pile it in
- Signed by founder when company is small enough to make that credible

---

## Variant B — E-commerce / physical product first order

**Subject:** Thanks — and here's what's coming next

**Body:**

> Hi [Name],
>
> Thanks for the [product] order — really appreciate you picking us.
>
> Quick rundown of what happens next:
> - **Tracking link** will land in your inbox the moment we dispatch (usually within 24h)
> - **Delivery**: [your standard delivery window]
> - **Before you start using it**: I'll send a quick "how to actually get the most out of this" note when it ships — it's a [type of product] and there are a couple of things that aren't obvious from the bottle/box
>
> If anything looks off when it arrives, just reply to this email — I'll be quick.
>
> — [Founder name], [Role] at [Business]

---

**Why this works:**
- Sets clear expectations on timing
- Promises a follow-up (the "how to use" email) — gives the customer a reason to expect future communications
- "If anything looks off, reply to this email" — direct line to founder is a quiet trust signal
- No marketing pitch, no "shop more" CTA
- Sets up the second email (which is where activation actually happens)

---

## Variant C — Service business first booking

**Subject:** Booking confirmed for [day] — what to expect

**Body:**

> Hi [Name],
>
> Confirmed for [day] at [time]. Here's what to expect:
>
> - **Where**: [address + parking notes if relevant]
> - **What to bring**: [if anything]
> - **What we'll cover**: [1-2 sentence overview]
> - **Reminder**: I'll send a quick text the day before
>
> If anything changes on your end, just reply or text — easier than going through the booking system again.
>
> Looking forward to it.
>
> — [Your name]

---

**Why this works:**
- Clear next steps reduce no-show anxiety
- Bullet structure makes it scannable
- "Reply or text" — opens the easiest possible reschedule path
- Mentions the reminder proactively (manages expectation about the day-before SMS)
- "Looking forward to it" — warm without being effusive

---

## Variant D — High-ticket / B2B first onboarding

**Subject:** Welcome — and our first 30 days together

**Body:**

> Hi [Name],
>
> Glad to have [Company] on board.
>
> Quick map of the first 30 days so you know what to expect from us:
>
> - **This week**: Setup call (15 min) — calendar link below to grab any slot that works for you and your team
> - **Week 2**: First check-in — make sure [the system / product / service] is fitting your workflow
> - **Day 30**: Honest assessment together — what's working, what isn't, what to adjust
>
> Calendar: [link]
>
> Direct line in the meantime: [phone or email]
>
> — [Your name], [Title]

---

**Why this works:**
- Maps the relationship's first 30 days — signals professionalism + commitment
- "Honest assessment together at Day 30" — pre-empts any "this isn't working" frustration that might build silently
- Direct phone or email is the B2B-appropriate gesture
- Title (not just name) signals seniority — appropriate for B2B

---

## Universal rules

- **Send within 1 hour of signup / purchase.** Anything later loses momentum.
- **ONE action / next step.** Multiple options dilute.
- **From a named human.** "[Brand] Team" emails get ignored.
- **Plain HTML, not heavily designed.** New customer emails should feel personal, not like a marketing newsletter.
- **Mobile-first.** Most opens are on phone. Short paragraphs, no wide images.

## When to customise

- **High-cost product / annual subscription** → make the email longer, more substantive. The cost justifies more communication
- **Freemium / trial** → emphasis on the value before the upgrade ask. Don't pitch the upgrade in email 1
- **B2B with multiple stakeholders** → send to the admin first, who then forwards to team. Don't try to onboard the whole team in one email
- **High-volume signups (consumer brand)** → cut to under 75 words. Less is more at scale

## What NOT to do

- Don't write "Welcome to the [brand] family!" — universally cringed at
- Don't say "We're so excited to have you" — the customer doesn't care about your excitement
- Don't ask for a review, referral, or follow-on-social in the first email
- Don't include 5 links to "explore" different parts of the product — overwhelming
- Don't include a celebratory image / gif unless your brand is genuinely playful
- Don't include a discount code "as a thanks" — trains future customers to expect them
- Don't have an obvious "unsubscribe" link as the only CTA at the bottom — even if technically required, balance with a real action
