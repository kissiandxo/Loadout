# DFY Template #5 — Win-Back Message

**Use when:** Reaching out to a churned customer who left 60+ days ago. Different variant per reason they left.

**Channel:** Email
**Length:** Short to medium (80-180 words depending on context)
**Calibrated for:** Honest, no "we miss you" sob-stories, no fake urgency

---

## Variant A — Price-driven churn (you launched a lower tier)

**Subject:** That price issue — we did something about it

**Body:**

> Hi [Name],
>
> Quick honest one — you cancelled [N] months ago and the price was part of why. Fair.
>
> [N] weeks ago we launched [Lite/Starter/lower tier] at $[X]/mo. It's the core features minus [specific things removed]. For [their use case / for solo users / etc.], it lands closer to where the value/price line should have been.
>
> Not asking you to come back today. Just thought you should know it exists.
>
> [Lower tier link]: [URL]
>
> — [Your first name]

---

**Why this works:**
- Acknowledges the price reason honestly
- Names the specific new tier and what's different about it
- "Not asking you to come back today" — lowers pressure, raises read-through
- Single CTA, low-stakes
- No expiry, no fake urgency

---

## Variant B — Fit-driven churn (came in, didn't activate, left)

**Subject:** I think we missed for you — but worth flagging this

**Body:**

> Hi [Name],
>
> Looking at the data, [Product] probably wasn't right for you when you tried us — you signed up but [specific behaviour suggesting low activation, e.g., "you didn't create your first project" / "you didn't make a second purchase"]. That's on us as much as anyone — we should have spotted earlier you weren't getting traction.
>
> If you'd be open to it: I'd love 2 minutes of feedback. What were you hoping [Product] would do for you?
>
> Even half a sentence by reply would be gold. No follow-up, no pitch — just trying to understand the gap.
>
> — [Your first name], Founder

---

**Why this works:**
- "I think we missed for you" — disarms the customer's expectation of being chased
- Honest about the activation gap they experienced
- Asks for FEEDBACK, not a sale
- Founder signature signals personal investment
- "No follow-up, no pitch" — promises a clean exit, which paradoxically lifts response rates

---

## Variant C — Neglect churn (was a customer for a while, drifted away)

**Subject:** It's been a while — wanted to flag something

**Body:**

> Hi [Name],
>
> Quietly noticed it's been [N] months since you were last active in [Product]. No pressure, no "we miss you" — that's not why I'm writing.
>
> Something we shipped recently might be relevant to how you used to use us:
>
> [Brief description of the new thing — 1-2 sentences. Must be genuinely relevant to their past use, not generic]
>
> If that lands as useful, here's where to look: [link]
>
> If not, totally fine — we'll quietly leave you alone going forward.
>
> — [Your first name]

---

**Why this works:**
- Honest acknowledgement of the time gap
- Explicitly NOT a "we miss you" email — names that this is different
- The new feature/update is specific and tied to their past behaviour
- "Quietly leave you alone going forward" — signals respect for boundaries, increases trust

---

## Variant D — Competitor switch churn (left for an alternative)

**Subject:** Quick honest one — about your switch to [Competitor]

**Body:**

> Hi [Name],
>
> [N] months ago you moved from us to [Competitor]. Honest question: how's it actually working out?
>
> Not asking to pitch you back — asking because [Competitor] is the alternative I lose the most customers to, and your specific take on what's working / not would be useful.
>
> Half a sentence by reply would be gold. If it's all positive, that's data too. If there are gaps, I want to know.
>
> If you'd ever consider revisiting: [link with one specific change we've made since you left]
>
> — [Your first name], Founder

---

**Why this works:**
- Refuses to pretend the switch didn't happen
- Asks for honest evaluation of the competitor (a request adults respond to)
- The CTA at the end mentions ONE specific thing that changed — addresses the "what's different now" implicit question
- Founder signature again signals personal investment

---

## Variant E — Life circumstance churn (job loss, business closed, etc.)

**Subject:** Just checking in — no pitch attached

**Body:**

> Hi [Name],
>
> Wanted to reach out personally — you mentioned [their stated circumstance, paraphrased gently] when you cancelled [N] months ago.
>
> No pitch attached to this email. Just wanted to ask how things are going on your side, and to make sure you know the door's still open if circumstances ever change.
>
> Your account data is still here on a paused basis (we hold it for situations like yours). If you ever want it back, just reply and I'll restore it.
>
> Either way, wishing you well.
>
> — [Your first name]

---

**Why this works:**
- References the specific circumstance respectfully
- Genuinely no pitch — sometimes the most powerful win-back move is no pitch
- Names the data-hold proactively — practical reassurance
- "Wishing you well" — human, not corporate

---

## Universal rules

- **Don't send to recently-churned customers** (less than 60 days). They're still in active disengagement
- **Don't send if they explicitly said "stop contacting me"** during cancellation. Respect the boundary
- **Don't send multiple win-back attempts within 6 months.** They'll filter you out
- **Don't use fake urgency** ("special win-back offer expires Friday"). Customers see it
- **Don't use the words "miss," "back where you belong," "family,"** or any other emotionally manipulative framing
- **Don't include a discount as the primary win-back lever** unless they specifically left for price reasons

## When to customise

- **High-LTV churned customer:** Make it personal. Send from the founder. Add a phone number.
- **B2B churn (a champion left their job):** Re-research the company; reach out to a new contact at the company with context, don't send to the dead inbox
- **Multiple win-back triggers fit the customer:** Pick the most likely one. Don't combine.
- **Customer left a negative review when leaving:** Different conversation. Handle the review first via the Review Response Generator, then evaluate whether win-back makes sense

## What NOT to do

- Don't write "We've missed you" — universally cringed at
- Don't use fake personalisation like "{{first_name}}, we miss YOU specifically" — reads as marketing automation
- Don't include "limited time win-back offer" — manipulation
- Don't pretend they were a power user if they weren't
- Don't include "follow us on Instagram" in a win-back email
- Don't run a 5-email win-back sequence. Three is plenty. After three, you're chasing
