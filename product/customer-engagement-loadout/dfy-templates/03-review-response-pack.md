# DFY Template #3 — Review Response Pack

**Use when:** A customer leaves a public review on Google, Trustpilot, Yelp, Facebook, or any review platform. Ready-to-adapt responses for 5-star, 1-star, and the 3-star middle.

**Channel:** Public review platform
**Length:** Calibrated per rating — 5-star (short), 1-star (longer to acknowledge what's real)

---

## Variant A — 5-star review response

**The 5-star review:**
> "Honestly the best customer service I've experienced from any [business type]. [Specific positive thing they mentioned]. Will be back."

**Response:**

> [Reviewer name] — that's lovely to read, thank you. [One specific reference to the thing they mentioned]. Glad we got it right for you.
>
> — [Your name], [Role] at [Business]

**Why it works:**
- Short — 5-star responses should be tight (under 50 words)
- Names them by first name (humanises)
- References ONE specific thing from the review (proves you actually read it)
- Signed by a real human with role
- No marketing pitch, no "share with a friend" CTA

---

## Variant B — 4-star review response (the gap is the lesson)

**The 4-star review:**
> "Great [product/service]. Knocked off a star because [specific complaint — e.g., shipping took 7 days, packaging was excessive, button was hard to find]. Would buy again though."

**Response:**

> [Reviewer name] — thanks for the honest note on [the specific issue]. [Acknowledgement of the gap in 1-2 sentences with what you're doing about it or what would change]. Glad the [product/service] itself worked for you.
>
> — [Your name], [Role] at [Business]

**Why it works:**
- Treats the missing star as the actually important feedback
- Acknowledges the gap specifically without defending
- If there's a real fix coming, names it (without overpromising timing)
- Doesn't get defensive about the part that's already working

---

## Variant C — 3-star review response (treat seriously)

**The 3-star review:**
> "[Mixed experience — some good, some bad, ultimately middling]. [Customer details what worked and what didn't]."

**Response:**

> [Reviewer name] — appreciate the balanced take. On [the negative thing they mentioned] — you're right, and we've [either the fix in progress, or honest acknowledgement that it's a known issue we're working on]. On [the positive thing] — glad that part worked.
>
> If you'd be open to it, I'd love to hear more about [the specific negative]. Reach me at [contact email] with "[Platform] - [Reviewer name]" in the subject and I'll respond directly. No pressure.
>
> — [Your name], [Role] at [Business]

**Why it works:**
- Treats 3-star as the most persuadable segment (which it is)
- Addresses BOTH the positive and negative specifically
- Offers private follow-up without insisting on it
- "No pressure" closes without being grovelling

---

## Variant D — 1-star review response (acknowledge what's real)

**The 1-star review:**
> "[Specific failure / experience]. [Often includes anger, possible threats — public review, refund, etc.]."

**Response:**

> [Reviewer name] — three things [or "two things", or however many they raised] went wrong here, and you're right to be upset:
>
> [List each specific failure they raised, in their words or close to. Take responsibility for what's real. Don't defend what's not.]
>
> I'd like to make this right directly. Reach me at [contact email] with "[Platform] - [Reviewer name]" in the subject and I'll handle it personally — [specific resolution within authority].
>
> — [Your name], [Role] at [Business]

**Why it works:**
- Acknowledges the specific failures by listing them — doesn't lump into "your experience"
- Takes responsibility without grovelling
- Doesn't offer compensation publicly (training future reviewers to leave bad reviews)
- Direct named-individual contact with a tracking phrase ("Platform - Reviewer name" in subject) so the email isn't lost
- Signed by a real human with specific role — signals personal accountability

---

## Variant E — 2-star with specific complaint (App Store / Play Store)

**The 2-star review:**
> "[App-specific complaint — usually a feature broken, crash, missing functionality]."

**Response:**

> [Reviewer name] — sorry the [specific feature/issue] is broken for you. We pushed a fix for [related issue] in version [X.Y.Z] [if true]. If updating doesn't resolve it, email [contact] with your device model and we'll look at it directly.
>
> — [Your name], [Engineering / Customer Care]

**Why it works:**
- Acknowledges the specific feature/issue (proves you read it)
- Names the fix version if applicable (concrete action)
- Names the device model — signals you'll handle the specific case
- Signed by engineering when the issue is technical — signals the engineering team is on it

---

## Variant F — Suspected fake / competitor sabotage review

**The review:**
> [Generic angry review, no specifics, from an account with no purchase history]

**Response:**

> [Reviewer name] — we don't have a record of any interaction matching this on our side. If you've been a customer and we missed serving you well, please email [contact] with your order details so we can look into it directly. Otherwise, happy to discuss whatever situation you're describing.
>
> — [Your name], [Role] at [Business]

**Why it works:**
- Doesn't accuse — even when you're certain it's fake
- Asks for verifiable information politely
- Signals to future readers that something might be off, without making the business look paranoid
- After posting, separately file removal request with platform

---

## Universal rules

- **Always respond from a named individual.** "[Brand] Team" responses feel automated.
- **Don't argue facts in public** even when you're right. Take complex situations private.
- **Don't offer compensation publicly.** Train your customer base wrong if you do.
- **Don't ask the reviewer to update or remove the review.** Trustpilot prohibits; Google flags.
- **Don't use the same response twice.** People comparing notice.
- **Don't respond when you're angry.** Wait an hour minimum.
- **Don't include emoji on negative reviews.** Tone-deaf.

## What NOT to do

- Don't write "I'm so sorry to hear that you had this experience" — most recognisable corporate script
- Don't write "We sincerely apologise for the inconvenience" — same
- Don't say "Please feel free to contact us" — useless filler
- Don't include marketing in the review response. They're talking about the experience, not the product
- Don't respond with a question only ("Could you tell us more?"). Address what's already there, then ask if needed
