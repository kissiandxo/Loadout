# DFY Template #2 — Refund Response Script

**Use when:** A customer requests a refund. Three variants depending on whether it's in-policy, edge-case, or out-of-policy.

**Channel:** Email
**Length:** 80-150 words depending on variant
**Calibrated for:** Approving cleanly, evaluating fairly, declining without damaging trust

---

## Variant A — In-policy refund (approve cleanly)

**Subject:** Refund processing — done

**Body:**

Hi [Customer first name],

All sorted — refund processing today. You'll see it in your account within 5 business days.

[For physical products:] No need to send the [product] back — keep it or bin it, whichever's easier.

[For SaaS:] Your access stays active until [end of billing period]. No further charges.

If anything comes up, reply directly to this email — it'll come to me.

— [Your first name]

---

**Why this works:**
- Decision is in the FIRST line, not buried
- No friction, no return form to fill out, no proof required
- Names the timing specifically (5 business days)
- "Reply directly to this email" — signals continued accountability without overpromising

---

## Variant B — Edge-case refund (out of policy, approve as goodwill)

**Subject:** Sorted — refund coming

**Body:**

Hi [Customer first name],

Technically your request is [N] days outside our [refund window], but given [the specific reason — defective product / shipping issue / your customer status], I'm waiving the window this time.

Refund processing today — 5 business days to your account.

Don't need the [product] back.

If [the underlying issue] is still there in any form, reply with the details and I'll dig in.

— [Your first name]

---

**Why this works:**
- Names the policy boundary explicitly without hiding behind it ("technically outside our window")
- Names the specific reason for the goodwill exception
- Frames as one-time ("this time") to protect the policy going forward
- Offers to follow up on the underlying issue — distinguishes care from policy compliance

---

## Variant C — Out of policy, decline (with alternative)

**Subject:** Refund — here's where I can land

**Body:**

Hi [Customer first name],

Honest answer: at [N] days past the [refund window], this falls outside what we can refund directly.

The window exists for a reason — [brief, honest reason — usually about how the product works, not "policy is policy"]. Going beyond it consistently would make the math not work for us at the scale we are.

Here's what I can do instead:

- **Store credit of [$X]** — usable on anything from us, no expiry
- **One-time exception escalation to [Manager name]** — if you'd like a human review, just say so, and they'll look at the specifics

If neither works for you, no hard feelings — I get it.

— [Your first name]

---

**Why this works:**
- Decision in the first line — doesn't make the customer wait through three paragraphs of softening
- Gives a HONEST reason for the policy (not "policy is policy" — actual reasoning)
- Two alternatives — store credit (immediate) and manager escalation (human review path)
- "No hard feelings" closes without being grovelling

---

## When to customise

- **Customer has high LTV / VIP status:** Default to Variant B (goodwill) even when Variant C would be technically correct
- **Customer is angry in tone:** Lead with one line of acknowledgement before the decision. "I can hear you're frustrated — let me address it." Then the decision.
- **Customer claims a defect:** No matter the policy window, send the goodwill version. The defect claim is separate from the refund window.
- **B2B customer / business context:** Slightly more formal. Replace "no hard feelings" with "I appreciate that may not be what you were hoping for."
- **Customer is leaving for a competitor:** Don't add a save attempt to a refund response. Refund clean. Save attempt is its own different conversation.

## Universal rules

- Don't require photos / forms / proof for trivial refunds — friction costs more than the refund itself in NPS impact
- Don't offer extended warranty / discounts / upgrades AT THE SAME TIME as processing the refund. Pick a clean exit
- Don't apologise for the policy itself — apologise for the customer's frustration if applicable, but the policy is fine
- Don't include marketing CTAs in a refund email. Reads as crass

## What NOT to do

- Don't write "I understand your frustration" — they didn't say they were frustrated. If they did, address it specifically
- Don't say "I'll need to check with my manager" unless you actually will. If you can authorise, authorise
- Don't process refunds at the END of the conversation expecting them to agree to other terms first. Process first, then any other conversation can happen
- Don't blame "the system" for slow refunds. The system is yours.
