# NPS / CSAT Follow-Up

You're following up on a survey response. The NPS / CSAT score the customer gave is the start of a conversation, not the end. Most businesses treat surveys as data collection and never reply — wasted opportunity. Each segment has a different unlock.

## NPS recap (in case the user doesn't know it)

NPS asks "How likely are you to recommend us to a friend or colleague?" on a 0-10 scale.

- **Promoters (9-10):** Love you. Will refer. Convert them into actual referrals.
- **Passives (7-8):** Satisfied but unenthusiastic. Convertible to Promoters with effort.
- **Detractors (0-6):** Unhappy. At churn risk. Recover them OR learn from their churn.

CSAT is similar but usually 1-5 stars on a specific interaction. The pattern is the same: 5 = promoter, 3-4 = passive, 1-2 = detractor.

## What you need from the user

- **The customer's score** (and which scale)
- **Any verbatim comment they left** (this is gold — comment > score every time)
- **Customer context** — name, status (new / repeat / VIP), what they bought, how long they've been a customer
- **What you can offer** — referral incentive (Promoter), a personal connection (Passive), a recovery gesture (Detractor)
- **Your name + role**

## How to respond by segment

### PROMOTERS (9-10) — convert love into referrals

The mistake most businesses make: a generic "thanks for the score!" The customer just told you they love you and would tell others. CAPITALISE.

**Three moves to make:**
1. Acknowledge specifically what they liked (if comment is given)
2. Ask if you can use their words publicly (case study, testimonial, social)
3. Make the referral path frictionless — give them something to share

**Template:**

> [Customer first name],
>
> A 10 means a lot — thanks. [One specific line referencing their comment if any.]
>
> Quick ask: would it be alright to use [the specific thing they said] in a customer spotlight on our site? No pressure, just say "no" if not — and we'd happily keep it anonymous if you'd prefer.
>
> Also — if you've got a friend / colleague / [relevant peer] who'd benefit from [your product], here's a referral link that gives them [discount or perk] and you [reward]: [link]
>
> Either way, glad we've earned the score.
>
> — [Rep name]

**When to vary:**
- B2B customer → ask if they'd be open to a brief LinkedIn quote or a case study, not a referral link
- E-com → straight referral link works
- High-LTV / VIP → consider asking for a video testimonial in exchange for a perk

---

### PASSIVES (7-8) — convert to promoters by finding the missing 1-2 points

Passives are the most overlooked segment because they're not screaming. But they're also the most movable. A 7 is satisfied but unenthusiastic — the gap between 7 and 9 is usually one specific thing you can fix.

**Two moves to make:**
1. Acknowledge the score gracefully (not "sorry it wasn't a 10")
2. Ask the diagnostic question: "What's the one thing we'd have to fix to make this a 9 or 10?"

**Template:**

> [Customer first name],
>
> Thanks for the 7. The honest question I'd ask: what's the one thing we'd have to fix — or do better — for you to give us a 9 or 10 next time?
>
> Genuinely useful for me to know. No expectation you have a polished answer — even half a sentence is gold.
>
> — [Rep name]

That's it. Short. Specific question. Most passives will reply. The replies often reveal a fixable product issue, a missing feature, or a comms gap you didn't know about.

---

### DETRACTORS (0-6) — recover OR learn

Two outcomes from a detractor follow-up. Either you save them (rare but possible) or you learn from why they didn't work (always possible).

The mistake businesses make: "Thank you for your feedback! We'll work on it!" → generic, deflating, makes the customer angrier. The detractor wants to be heard.

**Three moves to make:**
1. Acknowledge the score directly — "you gave us a 4" without softening
2. Ask the diagnostic — but with a humility frame
3. Offer specific recovery if there's a real fix available

**Template (for a 4-6, recoverable):**

> [Customer first name],
>
> A 4 means something went meaningfully wrong. Want to give me a sentence on what — even if it's something you don't think we can fix?
>
> If there's something I can put right, I'd like to. If there isn't, the feedback still helps. Either way.
>
> — [Rep name], [Role]

**Template (for a 0-3, deep detractor):**

> [Customer first name],
>
> A [score] tells me we've genuinely missed the mark for you. I'd like to understand what — partly to fix it for you if I can, partly because you took the time to score us and that feedback is worth more to us than a thousand "satisfied"s.
>
> 5 minutes on the phone or a couple of sentences by email — your call. My direct line is below if it's easier.
>
> — [Rep name], [Role]
> [direct contact]

Don't offer a refund or discount in the FIRST follow-up. Find out what happened first. Offering money before understanding makes the customer feel bribed.

## Worked examples

### Example A — Promoter with a specific compliment (9-10)

**User input:**
> "NPS came back: 10/10. Comment: 'Best customer service I've had from any e-commerce brand — Alex sorted out a delivery issue in under 2 hours and threw in a freebie. Will be back.' Customer is Jordan, 3rd order with us, $410 LTV. I'm Taylor."

**Suggested follow-up:**

> Jordan,
>
> A 10 means a lot — and Alex will love being named directly, so thanks for that.
>
> Quick ask: would it be alright to use the line "Best customer service I've had from any e-commerce brand" on our reviews page or social? No pressure, and happy to keep it anonymous if you'd prefer.
>
> Also — referral link below if you've got someone who might benefit. Gives them 10% off, gives you a $20 credit on the next order: [link]
>
> Either way, glad we've earned the 10.
>
> — Taylor, Customer Care

**Why this works:**
- Named Alex (per customer's request implicitly) — passes the praise along
- Quoted the most usable line back, asks permission to use it publicly
- Referral link inserted naturally — not begging
- "Either way, glad we've earned the 10" — closes gracefully even if they say no to both asks

**Next steps:**
- If Jordan grants permission → use the quote on Trustpilot / homepage / social
- If they share the referral link → confirm + apply the credit when used
- If they don't reply → no follow-up needed, the 10 is the win

### Example B — Passive (8/10), no comment

**User input:**
> "NPS came back: 8/10. No comment given. Customer is Mira, 2nd-time customer, $98 LTV. I'm Alex."

**Suggested follow-up:**

> Mira,
>
> Thanks for the 8. The honest question I'd ask: what's the one thing we'd have to fix — or do better — to make this a 9 or 10 next time?
>
> No need for a polished answer — even half a sentence is gold.
>
> — Alex, Customer Care

**Why this works:**
- Short. Easy to reply to. Single specific question.
- No "thank you for your feedback" filler
- The diagnostic question converts more 8s to 10s over time than any product change

**Next step if she replies with a specific issue:**
- If fixable → fix and email her back when fixed, with a "wanted to close the loop on this — you mentioned X and we've now Y"
- If not fixable → acknowledge honestly, offer one small gesture (free shipping next order, etc.)

**If she doesn't reply:** No further follow-up — she didn't want to engage. Note the 8 internally as a "monitor" — if her next NPS drops to passive again, that's a churn signal.

### Example C — Detractor (4/10) with specific complaint

**User input:**
> "NPS: 4/10. Comment: 'Product is good but the shipping took 12 days. Then the support team took 4 days to reply when I asked about it. Won't be back unless that improves.' Customer Sarah, first-time customer, $52 order. I'm Alex."

**Suggested follow-up:**

> Sarah,
>
> A 4 means we missed — and the comment tells me exactly where: 12 days to ship and 4 days to reply. Both are fair criticisms, and both are below what we should be.
>
> What I'd like to do, if you'll let me:
>
> 1. Confirm what happened on shipping (the courier window was tighter than usual that week, but we should have flagged it proactively to you)
> 2. Look at why support took 4 days — that's not where we want to be
> 3. Offer you a credit for the next order, no pressure to use it
>
> If you've got 2 minutes to reply with anything that'd help me understand the support delay specifically, that'd be gold. If not, I'll just take the criticism on board and we'll do better.
>
> — Alex, Customer Care Lead at Birchwood
> care@birchwood-organic.example

**Why this works:**
- Specifically names BOTH issues she raised — proves you read the comment
- Doesn't make excuses ("we had a difficult quarter") — just acknowledges
- Three concrete actions, numbered — feels like a real response, not a generic apology
- The credit offer is mentioned but de-emphasised — not the main thrust
- Direct email signature signals personal accountability

**Next step if Sarah replies:**
- Apply the credit immediately, ON HER ACCOUNT (so it appears automatically next visit, doesn't require her to remember a code)
- If she shares specifics about the support delay, investigate the root cause that day (within the support team)
- Send a personal "wanted to close the loop" email in 14 days

**If Sarah doesn't reply:** Apply a small store credit anyway ($15) — sends the signal that we acted on the feedback even if she didn't engage further. Don't email her to tell her about it — it's there if she comes back.

### Example D — Deep detractor (1/10), anger in the comment

**User input:**
> "NPS: 1/10. Comment: 'This brand is a joke. Wasted my money. Don't bother.' Customer Riley, first-time, $80 order. I'm Taylor. No prior support contact from this customer."

**Suggested follow-up:**

> Riley,
>
> A 1 with that comment tells me something went badly wrong, and I don't have visibility into what.
>
> I'd really like to understand. A couple of sentences by reply, or 5 minutes on the phone — your call. My direct line is below.
>
> If you'd rather just have your money back and walk away, that's fine too — full refund, no questions. Just reply with "refund" and it's done.
>
> Either way, no pressure. The fact that you took the time to leave the score and the comment means it matters to you, and that matters to me.
>
> — Taylor, Customer Care Lead

**Why this works:**
- Doesn't try to talk them out of the rating
- Doesn't try to defend the brand against "joke"
- Offers the refund as the easy path AND the conversation as the alternative
- "The fact that you took the time…" — names the implicit truth (truly indifferent customers don't bother to fill out NPS)

**Next step if Riley engages:**
- Just listen. Don't rebut anything. Acknowledge each specific point.
- If they ask for the refund, process it instantly — no friction
- If they share what went wrong, treat as gold for internal learning

**If Riley doesn't reply:** Process a goodwill refund anyway after 7 days. Communicate it briefly: "Decided to refund the order proactively. No reply needed. Hope your next product purchase serves you better." Costs $80, saves potential public escalation.

## Common edge cases

- **Score conflicts with comment** (e.g., 9 with negative comment, or 3 with positive comment) → trust the COMMENT, not the score. Some customers misclick or have inverted scoring assumptions
- **Customer scored once, then scored again differently** → most recent rules; reach out about the change ("noticed your most recent feedback was a 6 vs the 9 last month — what's shifted?")
- **No comment given on a low score** → can't go deep. Use the lighter "what's the one thing" template
- **Multiple-question survey, conflicting scores** (e.g., loved support, hated product) → respond to the LOW one. Don't dilute the negative with the positive
- **B2B customer with multiple stakeholders, mixed scores** → respond to each stakeholder individually with their score. Don't lump them
- **Promoter who hasn't bought in 6+ months** → reach out, but lead with a non-sales note. "Saw your old 10/10 — wanted to check in, no pitch attached"

## What NOT to do

- **Don't send "Thank you for your feedback! We'll work on it!"** This is the bottom of the response barrel
- **Don't ignore Promoters.** A 10 is an asset — extract value from it
- **Don't only follow up on Detractors.** Passives are where the biggest movement happens
- **Don't bribe Detractors before listening.** Money before understanding feels like a bribe and reads as guilt
- **Don't promise specific fixes you can't deliver** ("we'll have new shipping by next month") — vague directional commitments are fine
- **Don't ask Detractors to redo the NPS.** Score is what it is; don't fish for an update
- **Don't survey too frequently.** NPS quarterly max for repeat customers; once per transaction for CSAT
