# Plugin: Onboarding Maps

Pre-designed customer journey templates for the most common business types. Each map shows the sequence of touchpoints, the success metric per stage, and the most common drop-off points to defend against.

## When to use this plugin

Pair with:
- **onboarding-sequencer** (designing the actual messages for each touchpoint)
- **website-chatbot-builder** (chatbot interactions during the onboarding window)
- **faq-agent** (making sure the right FAQs are surfaced at the right time)

## What an onboarding map covers

A complete map includes:

1. **The customer's journey stages** — from purchase to habit-formation
2. **Touchpoints per stage** — what communications they receive
3. **The single success metric per stage** — the action that proves they've moved forward
4. **Drop-off points** — where customers typically disengage
5. **Defences against drop-off** — proactive interventions

## Map 1 — SaaS / subscription (consumer)

For products like Notion, Canva, Spotify, individual productivity tools.

### Stage 1 — First Use (Day 0)
- **Touchpoints:** Sign-up confirmation, first email, welcome modal
- **Success metric:** Customer completes their first meaningful action (creates first document / saves first item / etc.) within 5 minutes of signup
- **Drop-off risk:** Customer signs up + leaves without completing any action
- **Defence:** First-screen guidance is single, clear action. Don't show all features.

### Stage 2 — Discovery (Days 1-7)
- **Touchpoints:** Day-1 email, in-app tips, Day-3 email, Day-7 email
- **Success metric:** Customer returns 3+ times in the first week
- **Drop-off risk:** Customer doesn't return after Day 1
- **Defence:** Day-1 email is the critical touch. Highest-leverage email of the entire onboarding.

### Stage 3 — Habit formation (Days 7-30)
- **Touchpoints:** Day-14 email, in-app feature surfacing, Day-30 check-in
- **Success metric:** Customer reaches "aha moment" + has used the product 5+ times
- **Drop-off risk:** Customer plateaus at basic usage without discovering advanced value
- **Defence:** Day-14 email shows a non-obvious use case. Surface advanced features at the right moment, not all at once.

### Stage 4 — Activation (Days 30-60)
- **Touchpoints:** Day-30 email, optional Day-45 outreach
- **Success metric:** Customer has integrated [product] into their weekly routine
- **Drop-off risk:** Customer continues to use but doesn't deepen
- **Defence:** Day-30 email shows "what regular users do" — provides social proof + advanced patterns

### Stage 5 — Retention (Days 60-180)
- **Touchpoints:** Quarterly NPS, occasional value-add communications
- **Success metric:** Customer renews / continues to engage
- **Drop-off risk:** Quiet drift into Phase 1 churn
- **Defence:** Light-touch quarterly check-ins. Watch usage trajectory.

## Map 2 — SaaS / subscription (B2B)

For products serving 5-50 person teams.

### Stage 1 — Procurement & Setup (Days 0-7)
- **Touchpoints:** Sale confirmation, kickoff email, onboarding call invite (for higher-ticket plans)
- **Success metric:** Admin completes initial setup (workspace created, billing confirmed)
- **Drop-off risk:** Admin gets the product but never sets it up; team never gets invited
- **Defence:** Day-1 email goes to ADMIN with a single clear action: invite teammates

### Stage 2 — Team Adoption (Days 7-30)
- **Touchpoints:** Per-user activation emails, admin progress dashboard, Day-14 outreach
- **Success metric:** 50%+ of seats are actively used in first 30 days
- **Drop-off risk:** Admin paid for 20 seats, only 5 ever log in
- **Defence:** Surface team activation data to the admin. Cold seats are visible.

### Stage 3 — Integration into Workflow (Days 30-90)
- **Touchpoints:** Mid-quarter check-in, feature surfacing, success-stories from similar businesses
- **Success metric:** Customer integrates with 1+ other tool in their stack
- **Drop-off risk:** Product exists alongside but doesn't replace other tools — eventually deprioritised
- **Defence:** Show integration patterns with their existing stack. Make the connections obvious.

### Stage 4 — Champion Development (Days 90-180)
- **Touchpoints:** Quarterly business review (QBR) for higher-ticket accounts, case-study invitations
- **Success metric:** At least one user becomes a power user / champion
- **Drop-off risk:** Product is "used" but no one truly cares about it
- **Defence:** Identify the champion. Give them recognition, advanced training, beta access.

### Stage 5 — Renewal & Expansion (Days 180+)
- **Touchpoints:** 90-day-before-renewal outreach, expansion conversations
- **Success metric:** Customer renews + adds seats or upgrades plan
- **Drop-off risk:** Renewal becomes a budget question instead of an obvious yes
- **Defence:** Proactive value-proof report. Show what the team accomplished in the last year.

## Map 3 — E-commerce (one-time purchase)

For DTC product brands.

### Stage 1 — Anticipation (Order → Delivery)
- **Touchpoints:** Order confirmation, shipping confirmation, optional pre-delivery content
- **Success metric:** Customer arrives at delivery with positive anticipation
- **Drop-off risk:** Delivery delay without communication creates pre-arrival frustration
- **Defence:** Proactive communication about any delay BEFORE the customer notices

### Stage 2 — First Use (Delivery → Day 14)
- **Touchpoints:** Delivery confirmation, "how to use" content (Day 1-3), check-in (Day 7)
- **Success metric:** Customer uses the product as designed
- **Drop-off risk:** Customer doesn't open the box, or uses incorrectly
- **Defence:** Pre-receipt usage guidance helps. Day-7 check-in catches issues early.

### Stage 3 — Validation (Days 14-30)
- **Touchpoints:** Education content, "what to look for" guidance
- **Success metric:** Customer experiences the product's intended benefit
- **Drop-off risk:** Benefit isn't immediately obvious; customer assumes it doesn't work
- **Defence:** Set timing expectations. Vitamin C serums "take 4-6 weeks" etc.

### Stage 4 — Second Purchase Window (Days 25-60)
- **Touchpoints:** Reorder reminder timed to product usage cycle, NPS, review request
- **Success metric:** Customer reorders or refers
- **Drop-off risk:** Customer forgets / lapses
- **Defence:** Time-the-reminder-to-the-usage-cycle. "Bottle running low?" timing.

### Stage 5 — Brand Loyalty (Days 60+)
- **Touchpoints:** Quarterly content, new product launches, VIP recognition (for repeat buyers)
- **Success metric:** Customer becomes a repeat buyer + occasional referrer
- **Drop-off risk:** Customer drifts to a competitor with louder marketing
- **Defence:** Earn the loyalty through identity alignment — what does this brand stand for?

## Map 4 — Service business

For salons, clinics, agencies, consultants — businesses delivering ongoing service.

### Stage 1 — Booking & Anticipation
- **Touchpoints:** Booking confirmation, reminder (24-48h before), service-prep notes
- **Success metric:** Customer arrives with appropriate expectations
- **Drop-off risk:** No-shows
- **Defence:** Reminder mechanics (SMS + email), specific prep notes that signal professionalism

### Stage 2 — Service Delivery
- **Touchpoints:** In-person experience, follow-up note same-day or next-day
- **Success metric:** Customer leaves satisfied with the service itself
- **Drop-off risk:** Negative experience that doesn't get surfaced
- **Defence:** Same-day or next-day "how did that land" message — catches issues before they crystallise into a review

### Stage 3 — Between Services
- **Touchpoints:** Educational content related to the service, light-touch communications
- **Success metric:** Customer stays connected to the brand
- **Drop-off risk:** Customer forgets / a competitor reaches them
- **Defence:** Light cadence (not pushy), useful content (not promotional)

### Stage 4 — Re-booking
- **Touchpoints:** Timed reminder based on service cadence (e.g., 6 weeks for haircuts, 6 months for dentistry)
- **Success metric:** Customer books again
- **Drop-off risk:** Customer means to book but doesn't, then a competitor wins them
- **Defence:** Easy re-booking link in reminder

### Stage 5 — Long-term Loyalty
- **Touchpoints:** Recognition of long-term status, referral asks, occasional VIP perks
- **Success metric:** Customer becomes a regular + refers
- **Drop-off risk:** Customer's needs evolve and your service doesn't keep pace
- **Defence:** Check in on changing needs over time. Long-term customers in service businesses are gold.

## Map 5 — Hybrid (e.g., DTC product + service)

For businesses combining one-time products with optional services, subscriptions, or memberships.

This map blends elements of Maps 1 and 3. The key insight: customers often start with one path (e.g., one-time product purchase) and you want to migrate them to the other (e.g., subscription). The migration moment is its own stage and needs designing.

## Drop-off prevention — universal patterns

Across all maps, three drop-off interventions consistently outperform:

### 1. The "Day 1 nudge"
The single most important communication in any onboarding. Most customers either return on Day 1 and stay engaged, or don't return and churn. The Day 1 nudge converts the borderline cases.

### 2. The "Day 7 check-in"
Open-ended, low-pressure: "How's it going?" Catches confusion, frustration, and gaps before they become reasons to leave.

### 3. The "30 days in" graduation moment
Reframes the customer from "trying out" to "established user." Status reinforcement matters more than the practical content of the message.

## Common edge cases

- **Customer bought as a gift for someone else** → split path. Different onboarding for purchaser vs recipient.
- **Customer signed up but hasn't paid yet (free trial)** → trial-specific map with trial-end communication critical
- **Re-engaged customer (signed up before, churned, came back)** → SKIP earlier onboarding stages, jump to Stage 3+ — don't re-onboard people who've already onboarded
- **Customer who bought multiple plans / products simultaneously** → unified onboarding focused on the highest-engagement product first
- **Enterprise / large customer with multiple stakeholders** → role-specific onboarding paths (admin, end-user, executive sponsor — different content for each)

## What NOT to do

- **Don't send the same onboarding to every customer.** Segment by use case, role, or purchase pattern at minimum.
- **Don't measure onboarding success only by activation rate.** 30-day retention is the real metric.
- **Don't pack everything into the first 7 days.** Important features can be surfaced later when the customer has bandwidth.
- **Don't ignore the post-day-30 window.** Many businesses over-invest in the first 30 days and under-invest in the 30-180 day window where habit truly forms.
- **Don't run static onboarding flows forever.** Revisit quarterly. The product changes. The customer base changes. The flow should evolve.
