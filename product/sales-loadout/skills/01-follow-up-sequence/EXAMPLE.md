# Example — Follow-Up Sequence Skill

This is a real-shape example of how the skill should perform on a fresh prompt. Use it as a quality benchmark for your own outputs.

---

## What I typed into the project

> Need a follow-up plan for the Holland deal. They were here last Saturday with their two kids, test-drove the Outlander PHEV Aspire 7-seater (the white one). Husband Dan was clearly into it, wife Priya was the one with finance questions. Quoted them via email Sunday — $76k drive-away with the demo bonus. It's now Friday next week (so 6 days since the quote). No reply, no SMS, nothing. I'm Alex.

---

## What the skill produced

# Follow-up sequence — Dan & Priya (Holland family) · 2024 Mitsubishi Outlander PHEV Aspire 7-seater (white)
**Context:** Test-drove Saturday with kids in the car, quoted $76k drive-away via email Sunday, 6 days silent.
**Strategy:** Two decision-makers — Dan was emotionally bought-in, Priya was the finance gatekeeper. The sequence has to address her question stream first (touch 2), not just chase Dan's interest. Email-led because that's the channel the quote went on.

---

### Touch 1 — Day 6 (today) · Email
**Subject:** That white Outlander PHEV — anything I can clarify?
**Body:**
Hi Dan & Priya,

Just checking the quote landed and nothing got buried in your inbox over the week. Happy to walk through the finance side again with Priya whenever suits — I know that was the piece you wanted more detail on.

The white Aspire is still on the lot.

— Alex

---

### Touch 2 — Day 9 · Email
**Subject:** PHEV running costs — the bit we glossed over
**Body:**
Hi Priya,

Realised I didn't break down the running-cost side as well as I should have on Saturday. Quick numbers for the Outlander PHEV doing your school-run-and-back distance:

- 80% of your daily driving on electric only ≈ ~$3/day in home charging
- Petrol kicks in for the longer trips — averaging around 1.5L/100km combined
- Servicing is capped-price for the first 5 years

That's a real number difference from the regular Outlander you also looked at — happy to put a side-by-side together if it'd help.

— Alex

---

### Touch 3 — Day 12 · SMS
**Body:**
Hi Dan, the white PHEV is still here. Anything I can answer for you and Priya before the weekend?

---

### Touch 4 — Day 16 · Email
**Subject:** Should I pause this?
**Body:**
Hi Dan & Priya,

If the PHEV isn't the right fit after all, just reply "not now" and I'll close the file — no hard feelings, and thanks for the test drive on Saturday.

If it's still on the table but the timing's off (post-Christmas, after the bonus, etc.), tell me when's better and I'll quietly come back to you closer to then.

— Alex

---

### Touch 5 — Day 21 · Email
**Subject:** Final note — Outlander PHEV
**Body:**
Hi Dan & Priya,

Last message from me. If something shifted — different model, different timing, anything — I'm one message away. Otherwise, I'll leave it here.

Hope the kids enjoyed the test drive either way.

— Alex

---

**Notes for the rep:**
- Touch 2 is the high-leverage one in this sequence — it answers Priya's actual question and addresses her as the named decision-maker, which is the unlock here. Don't shortcut it.
- If you can get an updated running-cost or finance comparison from your BM before Touch 2 goes out, swap in the real numbers.
- If they reply at any point, drop the rest of the sequence and pivot to a live conversation.
- I assumed Dan's number is on file from the test drive — if not, send Touch 3 by email instead.

---

## Why this is a good output

- **Names the right decision-maker per message** — Touch 2 is addressed to Priya specifically, because she's the one with unanswered questions. Generic "Hi both" wouldn't move it.
- **The hook in Touch 2 isn't a re-pitch** — it's the running-cost detail that *they asked about*. New information earns a reply.
- **Touch 4 is the easy out** — and it's not softened. "Reply 'not now' and I'll close the file" is permission for them to say no, which lifts reply rates from people who genuinely won't buy but feel guilty ignoring you.
- **The mention of "the kids on the test drive"** in Touch 5 is a small human touch — costs nothing, signals memory.
- **Notes section names the leverage message** — Touch 2 — so the rep knows where to spend energy if they only follow up once.
