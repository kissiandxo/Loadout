# The Sales Script Vault

25 battle-tested scripts and message templates for the specific high-stakes moments that come up in dealership sales — the conversations the rep needs to nail in real time.

These aren't generic "objection responses." Each one is calibrated for a specific situation, designed to be deployed verbatim or adapted in under 60 seconds.

**Use these when:**
- You're mid-conversation and need a frame, fast
- The standard agents don't cover the niche-specific moment
- You want pre-built language for the scenarios you've personally lost deals on

## Section 1 — Trade-In Disputes

### Script 1.1 — "Redbook says my car is worth more"

> "Fair point — Redbook gives a band, usually trade-in low to private-sale high. Our offer is sitting at the realistic trade-in side of that band. Want to look at the full Redbook range together so you can see the spread? — Sometimes the high number people remember is the private-sale ceiling, not the trade-in floor."

### Script 1.2 — "My mate got more from [other dealer]"

> "Possible — different dealers price trades differently depending on what they need on the lot at that moment. Worth asking your mate what trim, kilometres, and condition his was — usually the gap closes when you compare like-for-like. If after that the numbers still don't match, I'd genuinely tell you to go with the better offer. I'd rather lose a trade than try to win one I can't justify."

### Script 1.3 — "I'd rather sell it privately"

> "Honestly? If you'd net $[private estimate] privately and we're at $[trade], you might be right to. Few things to factor though — most private sales actually clear 8–12% below ask, plus the cost of advertising, test drives with strangers, insurance gap during the change-over. Net to net, the gap usually narrows to $1k–$1,500. That gap is the cost of convenience and time. Worth weighing what your time's worth."

### Script 1.4 — "Why is the offer lower than what you'd sell it for?"

> "Fair question — every dealer makes margin on the spread between what we pay for a used car and what we retail it for. That margin covers reconditioning, detail, mechanical inspection, warranty cover, the lot fee, and the time it sits before it sells. The spread is usually 15–25%. If you'd rather skip that and capture it yourself, private sale's the way — happy to walk you through what's involved if it'd help."

### Script 1.5 — "Can you do better on the trade if I pay more for the new one?"

> "Sometimes — depends on which lever has more room. If we're already at the floor on the new car price, bumping the trade by $1k feels like a win for you but doesn't change the total deal. Let me get the BM to look at the whole picture and see what the cleanest structure looks like. Often the answer is a small move on both, plus an accessory throw-in, rather than $1k on one number."

## Section 2 — Financing Pushback

### Script 2.1 — "I don't want a loan, I'd rather pay cash"

> "Totally fair, and I'd never push someone into finance they don't need. One thing worth knowing — some manufacturer promotions have rates so low right now that financing actually costs LESS than the opportunity cost of pulling the cash from where it sits. Doesn't always apply, but happy to run the 60-second comparison so you've seen the math. If cash still wins, no harm done."

### Script 2.2 — "The weekly is too high"

> "Right — that figure sits on a [length] term at the indicative rate. Two levers we can pull without changing the car: stretching the term to [length + 1 year] drops the weekly by about $[N], or a balloon structure keeps the weekly low with a [%] lump sum at the end. Both have trade-offs. Want me to model both so you can see them at the same time?"

### Script 2.3 — "What if my application gets declined?"

> "Honest answer — the BM shops the application across multiple lenders, not just one. If the first one passes, we move forward. If they don't, the BM tries the next. Most reasonable applications find a home, but some don't — and if yours is in that group, we'd talk through alternatives like a co-signer, a larger deposit, or stepping into a different vehicle that fits the lending pool. No one's getting sold into something they can't sustain."

### Script 2.4 — "I want to use my own bank"

> "Smart play — relationship banks usually work in your favour. Two things to check before locking it in: (1) is your pre-approval current? Most are 60–90 days. (2) Does it cover the drive-away figure, not just the vehicle price? On-roads can add $5k+ and trip people up on collection day. If both are clean, your bank is the right answer."

### Script 2.5 — "I want to think about the finance overnight"

> "Of course. One thing though — the indicative rate the BM gave you is based on today's rate sheet. Lenders update Tuesday mornings, so the figure may shift by 0.1–0.3% by tomorrow. Probably nothing material — but if you decide to proceed, we'd run the application based on tomorrow's rate, not today's. Worth knowing so there are no surprises."

## Section 3 — Competitor Comparisons

### Script 3.1 — "I saw the [competitor model] for less"

> "Worth a look at — usually it's a spec-level difference, not a deal difference. Mind sharing the quote so I can do a like-for-like? I'll tell you honestly where we win, where they win, and where it's a wash. If after that they're still cheaper on the same spec, I'd rather you get the better deal than feel like we sold to you on bad info."

### Script 3.2 — "[Competitor] is offering [bonus offer]"

> "I saw that — that's their EOFY play. Worth knowing it usually comes with the trade-off of [the catch — locked finance, specific stock, time limit]. Ours doesn't have that string attached. So the headline numbers might look different but on full comparison, often closer than they appear. Want me to put them side-by-side for you?"

### Script 3.3 — "The [competitor] has better [specific feature]"

> "Genuinely yes on that one — they do. Two ways to think about it: how often do you actually use [feature] in real driving, and what does the rest of the package look like once you've decided you want that feature? Most cars give you one or two standout features and average the rest — our [model] takes a different approach, which is why the [feature you're better at] is where we win. Tradeoff conversation, not a winner conversation."

### Script 3.4 — "I'm going to test-drive the [competitor] before deciding"

> "Smart — drive both back to back if you can, that's where real differences show up. Couple of things to specifically check on the [competitor] test drive that often get glossed: [specific spec point #1], [specific spec point #2]. Not pushing you to pick ours — pushing you to drive theirs honestly. Then we'll talk again when you've done both."

### Script 3.5 — "Your dealership has worse reviews than [competitor]"

> "Yeah — I'll be straight, our online review average is [N], theirs is [M]. I can't undo that. What I can tell you is that I personally have [Y years] in this industry and a track record I'd be happy to point you toward — references, repeat customers in your area, anything. The dealership's average is the average; your experience comes down to whoever you're working with directly. Want me to put you in touch with a few customers who've bought from me specifically?"

## Section 4 — End-of-Month Closes

### Script 4.1 — Verbal-yes, paperwork stalled (SMS)

> Hi [Customer first name], quick one — got the [vehicle] all prepped for collection. Only thing missing is your green light on the docs. Any blockers I can help with? — [Rep first name]

### Script 4.2 — Manufacturer promotion ending (phone)

> "Hi [Customer first name], [Rep first name] from the dealership. Quick one — the [promotion] on the [vehicle] you're considering ends [day]. Don't want you to miss it if you're still planning to go ahead. Lock it in today or shall I just let you know when the next round of offers come through?"

### Script 4.3 — Customer's own deadline approaching

> "Hi [Customer first name], [Rep first name] from the dealership. Got the [vehicle] in the prep queue for [their deadline]. To have it ready in time, paperwork needs to be signed by [3 days before]. You comfortable coming in [day/time] to sign, or shall we book a different slot?"

### Script 4.4 — The "decision Friday" rep ask

> "Hi [Customer first name], [Rep first name] here. Honest question — are we doing this deal this week or not? I'm not pushing — I just need to know whether to keep the [vehicle] held for you or release it back to general stock. Either answer is fine, I just need an answer. Yes / no / not now is enough."

### Script 4.5 — Last-day-of-month softener

> "Hi [Customer first name], [Rep first name]. Wanted to flag — today's the last day of [month]. The pricing structure on the [vehicle] holds today; tomorrow it goes back to normal. If you're still in the maybe column, that's the only difference. If you'd already decided to wait, no problem — just wanted to make sure the decision was deliberate, not accidental."

## Section 5 — Late-Stage Ghost Recovery

### Script 5.1 — Day 14 silence after a quote

**Subject:** Should I pause this?

> Hi [Customer first name],
>
> Last message from me for now. I don't want to be the rep that won't take a hint.
>
> If you've decided the [vehicle] isn't the right fit, just reply "not now" and I'll close the file — no hard feelings.
>
> If it's still on the cards but the timing's off, tell me roughly when's better and I'll quietly come back into your inbox closer to then.
>
> Either way, thanks for the [test drive / enquiry].
>
> — [Rep first name]

### Script 5.2 — Day 21 silence (the deadline)

> Hi [Customer first name], one final note. The [held offer / stock hold / promotion] expires [day]. After that I'll need to release the [vehicle / colour / spec] back into general inventory. If you'd like to lock it in, let me know in the next [N hours]. If you'd rather not, that's the cue to close the file and let you get on with things.
>
> — [Rep first name]

### Script 5.3 — Day 30+ silence (the soft permission close)

> Hi [Customer first name], it's been a few weeks. Going to assume the [vehicle] isn't the right fit at the moment unless I hear otherwise. I'll keep you in mind if anything specific comes up that I think would suit, but I won't keep bumping your inbox.
>
> Thanks for considering us.
>
> — [Rep first name]

### Script 5.4 — Reactivation 60+ days later (with a real reason)

**Subject:** That [vehicle] — quick update worth knowing

> Hi [Customer first name],
>
> Long time no speak. Reaching out because [specific real change — new stock, finance market shift, EOFY, vehicle update, similar customer's outcome].
>
> If you've already sorted yourself out elsewhere, no worries at all — just say the word and I'll close the file properly. If you'd want to revisit, happy to send updated numbers.
>
> — [Rep first name]

### Script 5.5 — "I went with the competitor" — the learning ask

> Hi [Customer first name], appreciate you letting me know — congratulations on the [competitor model]. Genuinely curious, if you've got 30 seconds, what was the thing that swung it in the end? Pricing, the dealer experience, a specific feature, timing? — Purely for my own learning. No follow-up sales pitch, just want to understand. Either way, hope it serves you well.
>
> — [Rep first name]

## How to use the Vault

These scripts are templates, not lines to recite. Three rules:

1. **Read the script before you use it.** Make sure the situation actually matches.
2. **Customise the [bracketed] fields** with the real customer / vehicle / number / dealer name.
3. **Say it in your own voice.** Don't sound like you're reading. The script gives you the STRUCTURE; the delivery is yours.

The Vault is your tactical layer — the agents are your strategic and content layer. Together they cover roughly 80% of the high-stakes moments in a dealership sales role.

## What NOT to do with these scripts

- **Don't deploy them as a literal script** when you've never used the language before. Read them out loud once to yourself first — make sure they sound like you, not like a corporate sales training manual.
- **Don't run multiple Vault scripts at the same customer in a row.** They're calibrated for specific moments — overusing them in one conversation makes you sound rehearsed.
- **Don't use Section 5 ghost-recovery scripts on customers under 14 days quiet.** They're calibrated for the late stage. Earlier-stage follow-ups should be warmer; see the `/follow-up` agent.
