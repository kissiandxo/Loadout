# DFY Template #3 — Test Drive Follow-Up (Universal)

**Use when:** Customer test-drove a vehicle in the last 24–48 hours.

**Channel:** SMS preferred; email as fallback
**Length:** SMS ~3 sentences, Email ~5 lines
**Calibrated for:** The 48-hour reply window after a drive — single message, single ask

---

## Variant A — SMS (default)

Hi [Customer first name], [Your first name] from the dealership. Hope the rest of the [day they drove] was good. One thing I didn't get to mention — [specific detail tied to what they reacted to during the drive — boot space / tech feature / driving feel / running cost / etc.]. Thought of you with the [reason it'd matter to them]. Have a think, no rush — happy to answer anything when you're ready.

— [Your first name]

---

## Variant B — Email

**Subject:** That [vehicle] drive — quick follow-up

**Body:**

Hi [Customer first name],

Cheers for coming in for the [vehicle] drive [day they drove]. Hope it gave you a real feel for how it handles vs the [comparison they mentioned, if any].

Two things I forgot to mention:

- [specific detail #1 — relevant to what they reacted to]
- [specific detail #2 — relevant to a concern they raised]

If you want me to put together a side-by-side on [the comparison they're weighing], or a quick running-cost breakdown for [their use case], just say the word.

Otherwise, have a think and reach out whenever suits.

— [Your first name]

---

## Why this works

- **References the specific drive.** "One thing I didn't get to mention" is the highest-conversion opener for post-test-drive messages — it implies the rep is still thinking about THIS customer, not running a template.
- **Single ask, low pressure.** "Have a think" beats "want to come in tomorrow to finalise" by 5–10x on reply rate in the 48-hour window.
- **The "two things I forgot to mention" structure (email version)** gives you two narrow specific value-adds without dropping a re-pitch on them.

## When to customise

- **They drove with a partner/family who raised concerns** — send the email version, address the partner by name on one of the bullets. ("Two things — for [partner's name] specifically, [the data point that addresses their concern]…")
- **They explicitly said they're comparing with [competitor]** — replace one bullet with: "If you want, I'll send through an honest like-for-like comparison with the [competitor] — no spin, just where each one wins."
- **They drove and clearly weren't impressed with the model** — switch to the "wrong model" variant: "I heard you on the ride being firmer than expected — would you want to come back and try the [softer trim or different model] before we close the file on this one?"

## What NOT to do

- Don't lead with "Just wanted to follow up on your visit yesterday!" — generic, ignored.
- Don't include a quote in this message. Quotes belong in a separate dedicated email after a reply.
- Don't ask for the sale. The job of this message is the next conversation, not the close.
- Don't send after 48 hours have passed — the message no longer fits the window. Switch to the full follow-up sequence (`/follow-up`) instead.
