# DFY Template #5 — Ghost Follow-Up (the "should I close the file?" move)

**Use when:** A deal has been silent for 14+ days. You've already done 2–3 follow-ups. They're not engaging but you haven't been told no. This is the message that either revives the deal or lets you cleanly close the file.

**Channel:** Email
**Length:** ~80 words
**Calibrated for:** The "graceful exit" framing — paradoxically the highest-converting touch in any sequence

---

## The template

**Subject:** Should I pause this?

**Body:**

Hi [Customer first name],

Last message from me on this one for now. I don't want to be the rep that won't take a hint.

If you've decided the [vehicle] isn't the right fit, just reply "not now" and I'll close the file — no hard feelings, no follow-up emails, you can get on with your life.

If it's still on the cards but the timing's off (waiting on something, busy at work, partner's decision, etc.), tell me roughly when's better and I'll quietly come back into your inbox closer to then.

Either way, thanks for the [test drive / enquiry / showroom visit].

— [Your first name]

---

## Why this works (and the data behind it)

This template paradoxically converts higher than any other follow-up in the sequence. Three reasons:

1. **It gives the customer dignity.** Most people who've gone quiet feel mildly guilty about it. "Reply 'not now' and I'll close the file" gives them a respectful exit. Many take it. The ones who DON'T take it are surfacing themselves as still-real leads.

2. **It surfaces real timing constraints.** "If it's still on the cards but the timing's off" gives them a third option — not "yes" or "no" but "yes, but not yet." This option doesn't exist in most sales scripts and unlocks deals that were genuinely silent because of life circumstances, not lost interest.

3. **It de-escalates the relationship.** When customers feel pursued, they hide. This message announces "I'm stopping the pursuit." Counterintuitively, that's when they often respond — because the social pressure is off.

## When to send

- **Day 14–21 of a stalled deal cycle** — after 2–3 prior touches
- **As Touch 4 in a 5-touch sequence** (the `/follow-up` skill generates this for you, but you can also send standalone)
- **When you're about to give up on a deal** — send this as the "last ask" rather than ghosting them yourself

## Variations

### Variation A — For multi-decision-maker households

Change "Hi [Customer first name]" to "Hi [both names]" and the body's "your" to "you both." The dignity offer works the same; addressing both decision-makers explicitly makes it easier for one of them to forward to the other.

### Variation B — For fleet / B2B customers

Replace "thanks for the [test drive / enquiry]" with "thanks for considering us." B2B customers want process respect, not personal warmth. The body otherwise stays the same.

### Variation C — When you genuinely know the deal is dead

If you're sure they bought elsewhere or moved on, shorten dramatically:

> Hi [Customer first name], short note — if the [vehicle] isn't on the cards any more, no worries at all. Just reply "all good" or ignore this and I'll close the file. If you've gone with something else, I'd genuinely love to hear what swung it — purely for my own learning. Either way, thanks for the time.
>
> — [Your first name]

This variant pulls competitor-loss data more often than any other message, because the customer knows you're giving them an easy social out.

## What NOT to do

- **Don't soften "Should I pause this?"** as a subject line. It's deliberately curious and slightly disarming — softening it ("Quick check-in", "Following up") destroys the click-rate.
- **Don't send THIS message at Day 4.** Too early. It needs the prior touches to make sense. Touch 1 of a sequence should never sound like a goodbye.
- **Don't add a P.S. with a discount offer.** Mixing "I'm respecting your time" with "but here's $500 off" reads as manipulation. Keep the message clean.
- **Don't follow this with another follow-up if they don't reply.** The whole structural value of this message is that it self-terminates. Following up after means the message was a lie — which the customer can read 50 miles off.
- **Don't use this template for customers who explicitly asked you not to contact them.** That's a different situation entirely — close the file silently and respect the boundary.

## What to do AFTER you send this

- **If they reply "not now":** Send one short "thanks, will leave it here. Door's open if anything changes" and close the file. Don't continue.
- **If they reply with a timing hint ("ask me again in 2 months"):** Diarise that date in your CRM. Send NOTHING until then. When the date comes, send a fresh single message — not a continuation of the old sequence.
- **If they reply with a real objection ("we ended up going with X"):** Send a single warm "congrats" message, ask the learning question if appropriate, close the file.
- **If they reply with renewed interest ("actually, can we restart this?"):** Pick up where they are NOW, not where you left off. Re-discover requirements, don't assume the old quote still fits.
