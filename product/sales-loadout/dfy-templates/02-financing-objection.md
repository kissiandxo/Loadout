# DFY Template #2 — Financing Objection Response

**Use when:** Customer raises a finance-related objection — "I want to use my own bank", "the weekly is too high", "I'd rather pay cash", "I'm worried about the interest rate"

**Channel:** Email or in-person script
**Length:** ~120 words
**Calibrated for:** Reframing without arguing

---

## Variant A — "I want to use my own bank"

**Subject:** Your bank vs ours — quick honest take

**Body:**

Hi [Customer first name],

Totally fair to lead with your own bank — relationship banks usually work in your favour.

Two things worth checking before you lock that path in:

1. **Confirm your pre-approval is current.** Most are valid 60–90 days — if yours is older than that, worth re-confirming before we set a delivery date.
2. **Confirm the approval covers the drive-away figure, not just the vehicle price.** Some bank approvals exclude on-roads / dealer delivery / CTP — that gap can be $5k+ and surprises people on collection day.

If both check out, your bank's the right answer. Happy to deliver into your finance — just need the paperwork from your loans officer.

If anything shifts (rate, terms, timing), we can quickly compare against the dealer options as a backup. Either way you've got cover.

— [Your first name]

---

## Variant B — "The weekly is too high"

**In person / phone:**

> Yeah — fair. That figure is sitting in the [length] term at the indicative rate. We've got two levers without dropping the deal: stretch the term to [length + 1 year] which would drop the weekly by ~$[N], or look at a balloon structure which keeps your weekly lower with a [percentage]% lump sum at the end. Both have trade-offs. Want me to model both side-by-side so you can see them at the same time?

---

## Variant C — "I'd rather pay cash"

**In person / phone:**

> Sure — if the funds are there, cash keeps it simple. Worth knowing one thing though: some of the manufacturer finance promotions right now have rates so low that financing actually costs you less than the opportunity cost of pulling the cash out of [wherever it sits]. Doesn't always work that way — but worth me running the side-by-side for 60 seconds before you decide. If cash still wins, no harm done.

---

## Why these work

- **Variant A doesn't fight them.** It validates their preference, then surfaces the two things that genuinely matter (expiry and coverage). They feel respected; you're protected from a delivery-day surprise.
- **Variant B reframes from "high" to "structurable."** Most "the weekly is too high" responses are actually "I want a different structure" — give them the structure.
- **Variant C protects you from over-promising.** Don't claim cash is worse — give them a 60-second comparison and let the math decide.

## What NOT to do

- Don't pitch dealer finance vs their bank competitively. You don't know their bank's terms — you'll either lose credibility or get caught out.
- Don't quote a specific dealer interest rate as if it's confirmed. Always: "indicative until BM runs the application."
- Don't push extended warranty as part of the finance answer. Different conversation.
