# Plugin: Customer Profile Library

A reference of the eight most common dealership-customer archetypes — what they actually want, how they buy, how they react to pressure, and how to communicate with each. Loaded into a Claude project, this lets every skill's output be calibrated to the specific buyer type the rep is dealing with.

## When to use this plugin

Pair with every skill in the kit. Especially valuable with:
- **objection-prep** (Claude predicts objections by archetype)
- **negotiation-coach** (concession patterns vary radically by buyer type)
- **follow-up-sequence** (cadence and tone shift per archetype)
- **cold-outreach** (the hook differs per archetype)

When you start a deal, identify which 1–2 archetypes fit, drop a note in your Deal Summary plugin ("Buyer type: First-time + Reluctant partner"), and Claude will adjust automatically.

## The 8 archetypes

### 1. The First-Time Solo Buyer
**Profile:** First car / first major purchase / under 30 / often financing for the first time. Often coming in solo but mentally consulting parents/partners back at home.

**What they actually want:** A car they won't regret. They have low confidence in their decision and high anxiety about being upsold or making a mistake.

**How they buy:** Slowly. Lots of online research before walking in. Often visit 3+ dealers. Walk away from any high-pressure ask.

**How to talk to them:**
- LEAD with reassurance, not features
- Always offer the easy out ("Take it home, sleep on it, call me tomorrow")
- Don't run finance figures in the first conversation — quote drive-away only, then schedule a finance chat as a separate event
- Mention you're happy for them to bring a parent / friend / mechanic to the second visit

**What they react well to:**
- Honest acknowledgement that it's a big decision
- Specific running cost numbers (they want to feel "smart")
- Service plan information (they fear unexpected costs)

**Don't:**
- Don't say "great choice!" with high energy — feels like pressure
- Don't quote weekly finance figures unless they explicitly ask
- Don't push for a same-visit close — it terrifies them

### 2. The Skeptical Partner (Half of a Couple)
**Profile:** Came in with their partner. Their partner is the enthusiastic buyer; they're the gatekeeper. Often (but not always) the more financially conservative one. Often (but not always) the one who handles the family budget.

**What they actually want:** To not be steamrolled. To feel heard. To make a decision that's good for the household, not just the partner's happiness.

**How they buy:** They watch and listen during the first visit. They make the actual decision at home, hours after leaving. They're rarely the one who calls back — they're the one who tells the partner whether to call back.

**How to talk to them:**
- ADDRESS THEM BY NAME, not "you both"
- Answer THEIR specific questions in detail, not the partner's
- When discussing numbers, look at them, not the enthusiastic partner
- After the visit, the follow-up MUST address them specifically — generic "thanks for coming in" goes nowhere

**What they react well to:**
- Honest acknowledgement of their concerns
- Hard numbers (running cost, depreciation, fuel)
- Being offered the chance to lead the conversation

**Don't:**
- Don't pitch through them to the enthusiastic partner
- Don't try to handle objections as a husband-vs-wife mediator — let them have the conversation
- Don't write follow-ups that sound like they're addressed to the partner with a "P.S. Hi [skeptic]"

### 3. The Returning Loyal Customer
**Profile:** Has bought from your dealership before — sometimes multiple times. The car is at end-of-cycle (3, 5, 7 years). They liked their last experience enough to come back.

**What they actually want:** Recognition + the easy version of the process. They don't want to start from scratch. They expect a "thank you for your loyalty" element to materialise without them having to ask.

**How they buy:** Faster than a cold buyer. Often verbal-yes by end of first visit if the deal is in range.

**How to talk to them:**
- Acknowledge the loyalty upfront — "Welcome back, how's the [previous vehicle] been treating you?"
- Have their service history pulled before they sit down
- Make the trade-in conversation feel premium ("We know that car well — let me get you a number from the BM")
- Mention loyalty offers if they exist; if they don't, mention you'll see what you can authorise

**What they react well to:**
- A loyalty offer of any size (the gesture matters more than the dollar figure)
- "We have a [new model] just in — wanted to call you first"
- A streamlined process — short paperwork, pre-filled forms

**Don't:**
- Don't make them re-prove themselves like a first-time buyer
- Don't ignore them and let the floor manager handle it — that's an insult
- Don't quote a trade-in figure that's below market — they'll know and you'll lose them forever

### 4. The Fleet / Business Buyer
**Profile:** Buying 1–10 vehicles for business use. Tradie buying utes for the team, real estate agent buying for the office, plumber/electrician scaling up. Sometimes a corporate fleet manager.

**What they actually want:** Operational efficiency. Total cost of ownership. Vehicles that don't break down. A relationship with someone who handles the whole fleet, not just this purchase.

**How they buy:** Operational thinking, not emotional. They ask different questions: payload, towing, service costs, warranty terms, fleet pricing brackets, delivery timelines for the whole order.

**How to talk to them:**
- Talk to the BM about fleet pricing BEFORE the first meeting
- Lead with operational specs (payload, tow, fuel, service intervals)
- Have fleet pricing brackets ready (1–3 / 4–10 / 10+)
- Offer to deliver to their site, set up the whole fleet on one service plan, etc.

**What they react well to:**
- Genuine operational knowledge ("most plumbing fleets here run the GLS-R for the larger tray")
- Whole-of-life cost analysis
- A direct contact at the BM level, not just sales

**Don't:**
- Don't pitch features they don't care about (entertainment systems on a tradie ute)
- Don't quote single-vehicle pricing and expect them to imagine "× 5"
- Don't promise delivery timelines without checking stock at scale

### 5. The Cash Buyer
**Profile:** Has the money. Older, often retired or near-retired, often a second-car household. May or may not have a trade-in.

**What they actually want:** No haggling, no pressure, no finance pitch they didn't ask for, fair deal up front. Sometimes a "cash discount" out of principle (which usually doesn't exist).

**How they buy:** They've thought about it for months before walking in. The visit is a confirmation, not a discovery. Often verbal-yes on first visit if the deal is reasonable.

**How to talk to them:**
- Don't push finance unless asked
- Skip the upsells
- Be efficient — they didn't come in for a 90-minute experience
- Quote drive-away clearly and don't break it into weekly figures

**What they react well to:**
- Respect for their time
- Direct answers to direct questions
- Sometimes a small accessory inclusion as a "thank you for the clean deal" gesture

**Don't:**
- Don't push extended warranty / paint protection / service plans hard. One mention, no second mention.
- Don't pretend there's a "cash discount" — be honest that the deal is the deal
- Don't drag the paperwork — they came to buy, not sit

---

### 6. The Hagglers
**Profile:** Sometimes first-time buyers, sometimes seasoned car-buyers. Usually solo (couples balance hagglers out). Walk in expecting a fight. Often have a number in their head before they arrive.

**What they actually want:** To win. The deal matters less than the feeling of having gotten one over on the dealer.

**How they buy:** They open with a low offer, expect to negotiate up, will walk if you don't engage with the negotiation. They want to leave feeling like they earned a discount, even if the discount is symbolic.

**How to talk to them:**
- Don't get defensive
- Don't drop the price first — make THEM ask
- Trade levers: bump the trade-in or throw in inclusions before moving on headline price
- Let them have a small win — name a $200 accessory inclusion as "you've negotiated this"

**What they react well to:**
- Being engaged with as a serious negotiator
- A small symbolic concession that they extracted
- Closing in a way that respects their effort

**Don't:**
- Don't refuse to negotiate — they'll walk
- Don't give the biggest concession first — pace the moves
- Don't lecture them about "we don't haggle here" — even if it's true at your dealer, this customer won't believe you

---

### 7. The Researcher
**Profile:** Has spent 20+ hours online before walking in. Quotes you spec details verbatim. Knows the difference between trim levels. Often has a printed spec comparison sheet.

**What they actually want:** To be respected as informed. To find one thing you can tell them that they don't already know. A salesperson who doesn't condescend.

**How they buy:** Will walk if the salesperson seems less informed than they are. Decision is rational and slow.

**How to talk to them:**
- Acknowledge their research up front ("Looks like you've done your homework — what's the question I can actually help with?")
- Don't pitch features they already know about
- Offer specific real-world data your dealership has (your demo fuel economy, your stock turnover, your service customer feedback)
- Match their level of detail — don't dumb it down

**What they react well to:**
- A salesperson who admits "I don't know" once and then comes back with a real answer
- Specific data points they couldn't get online
- A test drive on roads that test the exact spec point they care about

**Don't:**
- Don't recite the brochure to them
- Don't say "great question" — feels patronising
- Don't make them sit through the whole walkaround if they've already done it themselves online

---

### 8. The Repeat-Shopper / Tire-Kicker
**Profile:** Has been "looking" for 6+ months. Visits the showroom every few weeks. Test-drove twice. Never commits.

**What they actually want:** Either (a) reassurance to finally buy, OR (b) the social experience of car shopping (some people genuinely enjoy this). Hard to tell which.

**How they buy:** Slowly, if at all. Half of these never convert. The other half convert when a real life-trigger forces the decision (current car breakdown, end of lease, family change).

**How to talk to them:**
- Be warm but don't over-invest energy
- Diagnose: gently ask "what's the one thing that would make this the right time?"
- If they can name a trigger, the deal is real and you can work it
- If they can't, they're not buying — be polite, stay in touch, but don't waste time

**What they react well to:**
- A relationship-based approach (texts when new models arrive, etc.)
- Honesty when you sense they're not ready ("Take your time, the car will be here when you are")

**Don't:**
- Don't run them through 7-touch follow-up sequences. They'll feel pursued and disappear.
- Don't try to manufacture urgency — they'll see through it
- Don't write them off completely either — half of them do eventually buy

## How to use this in Claude.ai

1. Drop this REFERENCE.md into your Sales Loadout project knowledge
2. When you start a deal, identify the archetype(s) in your Deal Summary plugin
3. Claude will calibrate every skill's output to that archetype automatically
4. Re-read the archetype yourself before the customer interaction — it's a 60-second prep that lifts conversion noticeably

## What NOT to do

- **Don't pigeonhole real customers.** These archetypes are starting points, not boxes. Real customers blend types.
- **Don't tell the customer their archetype.** "I can see you're a haggler!" doesn't go well.
- **Don't assume the archetype from appearance.** A 25-year-old in trainers can be a fleet buyer; a retiree in slacks can be a haggler.
- **Don't use these to manipulate vulnerable customers.** First-time solo buyers especially deserve the LEAST high-pressure version of you, not a tailored pressure approach.
