# Plugin: Vehicle Talking Points Library

Reference data for the most common vehicle categories sold at dealerships — the "what to lead with" and "what they'll push back on" for each. Designed to be loaded into a Claude project so the AI can produce talking-points-ready output for any vehicle the rep is selling.

## When to use this plugin

Pair with:
- **objection-prep** skill (Claude pulls category-specific objections)
- **sales-email-generator** skill (Claude leads with category-appropriate features)
- **proposal-builder** skill (Claude includes the right inclusions language for the category)
- **cold-outreach** skill (Claude's hook reflects the category buyer's mindset)

## How this plugin works

The library is organised by vehicle category, not specific make/model. For each category, you get:
- **Typical buyer profile**
- **What they really care about (vs what brochures lead with)**
- **Three highest-leverage talking points**
- **Three most common objections + the framing that defuses them**

Apply the category that fits the vehicle you're actually selling. The framings translate across brands.

## Category 1: Mid-size SUV (Sportage, CX-5, RAV4, Outlander, Tucson, Sorento)

**Typical buyer profile:** Family of 3–4, late-30s to 50s, replacing a sedan or smaller SUV, school-age kids, suburbs.

**What they actually care about:**
- Real-world boot space (after the dog / pram / hockey bag is in)
- Fuel economy on the daily commute (not the brochure highway figure)
- Ride comfort over Sydney/Melbourne/Brisbane road conditions
- Resale value at 5 years (they know they'll cycle out)
- The right number of cupholders (you laugh — they notice)

**Lead with (in this order):**
1. Specific boot capacity in litres with seats up AND with one row folded. "578L with seats up — easily fits the pram + groceries + a 7-year-old's hockey kit."
2. Real-world fuel consumption from your own demo drives, not the official combined figure. "I've been driving the demo for 3 weeks doing mostly school-run-and-back; I'm averaging 7.2L/100km."
3. Safety rating + the specific feature that matters for school-run age kids (rear cross-traffic alert, blind-spot monitoring, AEB at low speeds).

**Top objections:**
- *"It's bigger than I'm used to"* → Reframe to specific dimensions vs their current car. Often the perception is wider than reality.
- *"The fuel economy worries me"* → Use your real numbers, not the brochure's. Brochure numbers always feel like marketing.
- *"What about depreciation?"* → Show resale value of the 3-year-old version on Carsales right now. Specific numbers beat reassurance.

## Category 2: Large 7-seater SUV (Carnival, Pathfinder, Pajero Sport, Sorento, Outlander 7-seater)

**Typical buyer profile:** 3+ kids, grandparents on board occasionally, weekend road trips, regional driving common.

**What they actually care about:**
- Third-row legroom WITH second row in a normal position (not pushed forward)
- Boot space WITH all three rows up
- Towing capacity (boat, caravan, trailer) — even if they don't currently tow
- The kids' fight prevention features (separate climate zones, USB ports per seat, third-row air vents)
- Insurance cost (often jumps significantly vs mid-size SUVs)

**Lead with:**
1. Demonstrate the third row with someone sitting in the second row in a normal driving position. Don't show row 3 with row 2 pushed forward — that's the brochure photo trick.
2. Boot capacity with all three rows up + a specific real-world equivalent ("That's 285L — enough for the weekly shop or 4 cabin bags").
3. The "kid management" tech: zones, USB-per-seat, rear sunshades, AEB rear, child-lock anchors.

**Top objections:**
- *"It might be too big to park"* → Always demonstrate the reversing camera + 360-view + park-assist on the test drive. Make it the first thing they try.
- *"We only need 7 seats sometimes"* → Reframe to second-row comfort. With the third row stowed, the row 2 experience in a 7-seater beats a 5-seater of the same length.
- *"Fuel costs scare me"* → For hybrid/PHEV variants, run the school-run-day vs road-trip-day calc separately. Most families' weekly drives are 80% short trips where the hybrid economics work hard.

## Category 3: Mid-size ute (Hilux, Ranger, Triton, D-Max, BT-50, Amarok)

**Typical buyer profile:** Two distinct sub-groups — (a) work-fleet buyers (tradies, builders, fleet managers) and (b) lifestyle dual-cab buyers (families with boats / camping / 4WD weekends).

**What they actually care about:**
- **Tradies:** Payload (kg), tray dimensions (will my gear fit), reliability reputation, monthly fuel bill on long-distance work.
- **Lifestyle buyers:** Towing capacity (3,500kg is the magic number), tub size for camping setups, ride comfort unladen (utes drive harshly without load), the in-cabin experience because the wife/family will be in it too.

**Lead with (different per sub-group):**

For tradies:
1. Payload number + tray dimensions. "1,000kg payload, 1,520mm tub length — fits a standard-size pallet."
2. Service intervals + cost of capped-price servicing. "$XXX every 15,000km, 7 years capped."
3. The reliability conversation honestly. Don't bash the competition — speak to the model's actual track record.

For lifestyle buyers:
1. The 3,500kg towing capacity and the tow-tech (trailer sway control, integrated trailer brake, etc.).
2. The unladen ride comfort + cabin tech (Apple CarPlay, cooled seats if available, decent audio).
3. The wife/family conversation. Most lifestyle utes are sold once the partner is sold. Address them as a buyer.

**Top objections:**
- *"It's a work vehicle — does it feel nice enough for daily?"* (lifestyle buyer) → Test drive on a non-construction-site road. Most utes have closed the gap on car-like ride.
- *"The Hilux has the reputation"* (Triton/D-Max/BT-50 reps) → Acknowledge, then surface the spec difference. The reputation premium often costs $5k+ in real terms.
- *"Will it actually carry [specific load]?"* → Don't BS. Get the load weighed if they're unsure. Specific honesty beats generalised assurances.

## Category 4: Compact car (Cerato, i30, Mazda 3, Corolla, Polo)

**Typical buyer profile:** First-time buyer / student / second household car / fleet vehicle. Budget-sensitive, fuel-sensitive, insurance-sensitive.

**What they actually care about:**
- Drive-away price + weekly finance figure
- Fuel economy real-world
- Insurance cost
- The infotainment / phone integration (mostly young buyers)
- Resale at 5 years

**Lead with:**
1. The total cost of ownership picture, not just the headline price. Weekly fuel + weekly finance + insurance per month.
2. Real fuel economy you've achieved on demos.
3. The CarPlay / Android Auto / wireless charging features — these matter disproportionately to young buyers.

**Top objections:**
- *"It's at the top of my budget"* → Reframe to weekly cost. Most first-time-buyers think in monthly/weekly, not total.
- *"My parents say I should get a [different model]"* → Don't argue. Suggest the parent comes in for the second visit.
- *"I'd rather buy used"* → Run the new-vs-used economics honestly. With low rates, new often wins on TCO; with high rates, used wins. Don't blanket-claim.

## Category 5: EV / PHEV (EV5, EV9, BYD Atto, Tesla Model Y, Outlander PHEV, RAV4 hybrid)

**Typical buyer profile:** Range from environmentally-motivated to financially-motivated to early-adopter status-motivated. Often have done extensive online research before walking in.

**What they actually care about:**
- Range (WLTP and real-world both — they often know the gap)
- Charging — at home, on road trips, charger network availability
- Battery warranty + degradation over 5–8 years
- Running cost vs petrol equivalent in actual numbers
- (For PHEV specifically) Daily commute electric-only feasibility

**Lead with:**
1. Real-world range numbers, not WLTP. Be honest about the 20–25% reduction in highway driving.
2. The home-charging story. Most concerns dissolve when you walk them through "you wake up to a full battery every day."
3. The running cost math, in dollars per week, against their current car. Use their actual postcode + driving pattern, not generic numbers.

**Top objections:**
- *"What about long road trips?"* → Have a real charging route plan ready for their actual destinations (Plugshare or Chargefox). Don't generalise.
- *"What about battery degradation in 7 years?"* → Address with the actual warranty + the resale data on 5-year-old Teslas/Leafs. Numbers beat anxiety.
- *"They're going to make better ones next year"* → True, and same applies to phones. Doesn't change the value proposition today.

## How to use this in Claude.ai

1. Drop this REFERENCE.md into your Sales Loadout project knowledge
2. When you invoke any skill, Claude will reach for the right category automatically based on the vehicle mentioned
3. Claude will use the talking points to ground its output in dealer-realistic language, not generic AI marketing-speak

## What NOT to do

- Don't recite the talking points to the customer like a script. They're prompts for YOUR thinking, not lines for delivery.
- Don't lead with the same talking point regardless of customer. A young first-time buyer and a fleet manager need completely different opens — this plugin is a menu, not a recipe.
- Don't claim category averages as your dealership's specific reality. If the brochure says 7.0L/100km and your demo drives at 8.5L/100km, the truth is 8.5L. Lying about fuel economy is the fastest way to lose trust on day 1 of ownership.
