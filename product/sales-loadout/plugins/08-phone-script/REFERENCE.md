# Plugin: Phone Script Library

Reference scripts for the phone calls that dealership reps avoid most — and exactly the calls that move deals. Phone is the highest-conversion channel in the entire sales funnel and the one reps default away from for email. Loaded into a Claude project, this library gives Claude the structure to produce phone scripts that don't sound canned.

## When to use this plugin

Pair with:
- **follow-up-sequence** — Touch 3 is often more powerful by phone than SMS
- **objection-handler** — phone is often the right move for high-emotion objections
- **no-show-recovery** — Touch 1 same-day is increasingly phone-first
- **priority-list** — when the day's #1 action is "call X"

## Why phone scripts matter

Reps avoid phone calls because they're emotionally harder than emails. Emails can be drafted, edited, sent and forgotten. Phone calls require improvisation, real-time reaction, voice tone, the risk of voicemail awkwardness.

The honest data: a 90-second phone call has 5–8x the response yield of a 200-word email at the same stage of a deal. Phone calls also dramatically reduce the "ghosting" rate at Day 7–14 post-quote.

The scripts below aren't word-for-word recitations. They're STRUCTURE — what to open with, how to handle the most likely response paths, how to end. Read them before the call, then talk like yourself.

## Script 1: Quote follow-up at Day 4–7

**Context:** You sent a quote on Day 0. Customer hasn't replied. It's now Day 5. Phone is the right move.

**Opening:**
> "Hi [Name], it's [Rep] from [Dealership]. Got a quick minute? — Don't worry, this isn't a sales pitch. I just want to check if the quote I sent on [day] landed alright, or whether you had any questions on it that I missed."

**If they say "I'm just thinking about it":**
> "Of course, no rush at all. Out of curiosity — is there a specific bit you're thinking about? The figure, the colour, the timing? Three different conversations and I might be able to help with one of them."

**If they say "I'm comparing it with [X]":**
> "Totally fair — what are the two or three things you're comparing on? I can be honest about where we'd win or lose against the [X], so you've got an actual comparison rather than two brochures."

**If they say "I'll get back to you next week":**
> "Sounds good. Quick one — should I follow up next [day], or would you rather I just leave it with you and you reach out when you're ready?"
[The "would you rather I leave it with you" option is what makes this script work. It gives them control. Most will say "no, follow up next week" — and now you have permission.]

**If they don't answer (voicemail):**
> "Hi [Name], [Rep] from [Dealership]. Just calling to check the quote landed — no rush, no pressure. If you want to text or email back when convenient, that's perfectly fine. Talk soon."
[Don't try to handle the whole sales pitch in voicemail. The voicemail's job is to make the next attempt non-awkward.]

**Closing the call:**
> "Right — I'll [agreed action]. Cheers, [Name]."

## Script 2: No-show recovery, same-day

**Context:** Customer booked a 10am test drive / appointment, didn't show. It's now 12pm same day. You're calling.

**Opening:**
> "Hi [Name], it's [Rep] from [Dealership]. No drama at all about this morning — just wanted to check you're okay and see if you'd like to rebook."

**If they apologise:**
> "Honestly, don't worry — it happens. The [vehicle] is still here. When would suit you better — sometime later this week, weekend, or shall we try again next week?"

**If they say "I've decided not to":**
> "Fair enough. Any feedback for me — was it the model, the price, or just timing? Anything you tell me is useful, and there's no pressure to elaborate."

**If they don't answer:**
> [Voicemail script]: "Hi [Name], [Rep] from [Dealership]. No worries about the missed appointment — life happens. The [vehicle] is still here when you are. Text or call back whenever suits, or I'll send a quick text after this so you've got my number."

## Script 3: Verbal-yes confirmation call

**Context:** Customer said "yes" verbally 3+ days ago. Paperwork or finance is in motion. They haven't confirmed details for collection. Verbal-yes deals collapse on silence — this is the call that prevents that.

**Opening:**
> "Hi [Name], [Rep] from [Dealership]. Just a quick update on the [vehicle] — finance application is [status], we're aiming for [day] collection. Wanted to confirm that works for you and answer anything that's come up in the meantime."

**If they have new questions:**
[Answer directly. Don't redirect to email. Their willingness to ask new questions is the unlock — handle it now.]

**If they sound hesitant or quieter than expected:**
> "Get the sense something's shifted since we spoke — is there anything bugging you about it that I can address now while it's easy?"
[CRITICAL: surface hesitation BEFORE collection day. Cold feet on collection day is the worst place for it.]

**If they're enthusiastic:**
> "Perfect — I'll confirm the time with you on [day before collection] and we'll have everything ready. Want me to send the final paperwork list now or in a couple of days?"

## Script 4: The "I'm thinking about it" customer at Day 10+

**Context:** Quoted, customer said "I want to think about it," 10+ days have passed, no movement.

**Opening:**
> "Hi [Name], [Rep] from [Dealership]. Don't want to be the rep that keeps chasing — just wanted to check whether the [vehicle] is still on the table for you, or whether you've decided to go a different direction. Either's fine, I just want to know whether to hold the spot or close the file."

**If "still thinking":**
> "Fair. Let me ask the awkward question — what would actually move you off the fence? More time, a different angle on the figures, a second drive, a finance comparison? Sometimes the thinking gets stuck and a small thing unsticks it."

**If "I've decided to wait":**
> "All good. Want me to leave the door open for, say, end of [period], and check back then? Or just close it down completely?"

**If "I bought elsewhere":**
> "Ah, no worries — congrats on the new car. Genuinely. If you've got 30 seconds, what was the thing that swung it? — Helps me for next time."
[The most under-used data point in dealership sales is the honest answer to "why did you go elsewhere?" If you can get it, you've earned data worth far more than the lost deal.]

## Script 5: The "out of nowhere" reactivation call

**Context:** A lead from 6+ months ago. They never bought. You haven't talked since. You have a real reason to call — new stock, a trim change, a finance market shift, a vehicle anniversary.

**Opening:**
> "Hi [Name], it's [Rep] from [Dealership] — long time no speak. I'll be quick — [the specific reason] just came up and I thought of you. Got a minute or shall I call back?"

**If "got a minute":**
> [Deliver the reason in 2 sentences. Then:] "No pressure either way — happy to send you the details by email if it's something to revisit, or close the file if you've already sorted yourself out elsewhere."

**If "I bought elsewhere":**
> "All good — appreciate you taking the call. Mind if I ask what you ended up with?"
[Listen, take notes for the CRM. Don't pitch.]

**If they're interested:**
> "Brilliant — let me send through the details this afternoon and we can chat properly when it's easier. What's the best email?"

## General phone call discipline

**Before you call:**
- Have the customer's deal context open (Deal Summary plugin filled in)
- Know your one ask for the call (what's the "yes" you're after?)
- Decide how long you'll stay on if it's a voicemail (90 seconds max)

**During the call:**
- Smile — you can hear it in someone's voice
- Talk slower than you naturally would; high-emotional contexts benefit from pace
- Pause after questions. Don't fill silence — the customer's filling is more valuable than yours
- Take notes during, not after

**After the call:**
- Update the CRM within 5 minutes, not later
- Send the agreed follow-up (email, text, calendar invite) within the hour
- Diarise the next contact point

## How to use this in Claude.ai

1. Drop this REFERENCE.md into your Sales Loadout project knowledge
2. When you ask Claude for "a phone script for the [name] follow-up" or "what to say when I call Sarah", it will pull from these patterns and adapt to your deal context
3. Read the script back to yourself before dialling — out loud once — and you'll deliver it more naturally

## What NOT to do

- **Don't read the script word-for-word.** It's structure, not lines. Talk like yourself.
- **Don't make the call after 8pm or before 8am** unless explicitly requested.
- **Don't leave more than ONE voicemail per attempt cycle.** Two voicemails in a day reads as desperate.
- **Don't pitch on a callback.** If they're returning a missed call, the first 30 seconds are theirs, not yours.
- **Don't ask "is now a good time?" as the first sentence.** It primes a "no." Ask after introducing yourself + giving them the reason.
- **Don't make the call when you're frustrated.** Your tone leaks. Wait 10 minutes.
